About Me

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Applied Mathematics at The University of Arizona, supervised by Dr. Michael Chertkov and Dr. Laurent Pagnier . My research focuses on mathematical modeling, uncertainty quantification, probabilistic risk assessment, stochastic simulation, and computational methods for complex dynamical and networked systems, with a particular emphasis on electric power systems and interdependent energy networks.

I develop mathematical and computational methods for studying the behavior, stability, and resilience of large-scale systems under uncertainty. My work combines probability, stochastic processes, dynamical systems, optimization, rare-event simulation, statistical inference, and high-performance scientific computing. I am particularly interested in translating theoretical ideas into scalable computational frameworks that can be used to analyze realistic, data-intensive systems.

My research has included collaborations with Los Alamos National Laboratory, the University of Arizona, and the Israel Electric Corporation through work involving NOGA, the Israeli Independent System Operator. These experiences have allowed me to work across academic, national laboratory, and industry-oriented research environments.

Alongside my work in energy systems, I am increasingly interested in machine learning, artificial intelligence, data-driven modeling, and optimization. In particular, I am interested in understanding how modern statistical and machine learning methods can be combined with mathematical modeling and scientific computing to address challenging problems in complex physical systems.

Research Projects

Real-Time Stochastic Assessment of Dynamic N-1 Grid Contingencies

This project develops a computational framework for analyzing the dynamic response of power transmission networks under N-1 contingency scenarios, with a focus on transient stability and line overheating risk. The framework combines analytical solutions, stochastic differential equations, Monte Carlo simulation, and importance sampling to identify vulnerable network components and estimate probabilistic system risk under randomized fault scenarios.

Project Repository

System-Agnostic Localization of Oscillations (SALO)

This project implements the System-Agnostic Localization of Oscillations algorithm for identifying the origin and frequency of forced oscillations in complex dynamical networks. The approach combines spectral analysis, Fourier methods, maximum likelihood estimation, and optimization to infer forcing characteristics from observable time-series data.
